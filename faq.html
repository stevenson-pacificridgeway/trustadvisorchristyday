Living Trust Questions, Answered

Straight answers about trusts, wills, and probate in California.

Home › FAQ
What is a living trust?

A revocable living trust is a legal arrangement that holds your assets during your life and passes them to your chosen beneficiaries at death — without probate court. You stay in control as trustee and can change it any time.

Do I need a living trust in California?

If you own a home or have more than $208,850 in assets outside of beneficiary designations, your family will generally face probate without one. That's the California small-estate limit as of April 1, 2025.

How long does probate take in California?

Full probate typically takes 12 to 18 months, and can take longer if there are disputes or real estate sales. Trust administration often finishes in a few months.

How much does probate cost in California?

Attorney and executor fees are each set by statute as a percentage of the gross estate: 4% of the first $100,000, 3% of the next $100,000, and 2% of the next $800,000. A $1,000,000 estate means about $46,000 in combined statutory fees before other costs. Try our probate cost calculator.

Is a will enough?

A will names who inherits and who should raise your children, but in California it doesn't avoid probate for estates over $208,850. Most homeowners pair a living trust with a pour-over will.

What's included in your estate plan?

An attorney-prepared revocable living trust, pour-over will with guardianship nomination, financial and healthcare powers of attorney, advance healthcare directive and HIPAA release, notarization, deed transfer of your home into the trust, and funding guidance.

How long does it take to set up a trust?

Most clients complete their entire living trust in about 2–3 weeks, from the first call to signing.

Will putting my house in a trust raise my property taxes?

Transferring your home into your own revocable living trust typically does not trigger a Prop 13 reassessment in California.

Can I change my trust later?

Yes. A revocable living trust can be amended or revoked at any time while you're living and competent. We recommend reviewing it after major life events.

What does it mean to fund a trust?

Funding means retitling assets — like your home and bank accounts — into the name of the trust. An unfunded trust doesn't avoid probate, which is why funding guidance and the deed transfer are included.

Do young adults need a power of attorney?

Once a child turns 18, parents lose automatic authority over medical and financial matters. A young adult healthcare and financial POA lets a trusted parent step in during an emergency.
